Executive Summary

What We Found

The site is full of real value — but that value is buried under too many choices, too little story, and a design that hasn't changed in 15 years.

Associated Training Services (ATS) has something powerful: 50+ years of operation, accredited crane and heavy equipment programs, real job placement help, and graduates earning $26/hr or more. The problem is none of that comes through in the first 30 seconds on the site.

A visitor lands on the homepage and sees a dark sidebar with 20+ menu items, six competing buttons, and a "Hiring Instructors" CTA aimed at job seekers — not the student who came to change their career. The person who should become a student leaves before they ever feel understood.

Copy Audit · Score: 20/100

What the Words Are Doing Wrong

The site describes programs. It never sells a future. Every line is about ATS — none of it is about the person reading.

Critical
No hero headline. "Associated Training Services" is a company name, not a promise.
The first thing a visitor reads is the company name. It tells them nothing about what changes for them if they enroll. The student asking "can I make a real living doing this?" gets no answer above the fold.
Critical
The best proof is at the bottom — in testimonials no one reaches.
Greg Martineau earns $26/hr running a 50-ton crane. That is a life-changing number. It appears in tiny text near the footer. It should be the centerpiece of the hero. Specific wages are the most persuasive thing on the entire site.
High
"Click here" is used throughout as link text.
"Click here" is one of the weakest phrases in copywriting. It is generic and adds no motivation. Every CTA should describe the outcome: "Get Your Free Career Kit" or "Start Your Application" moves people. "Click here" does not.
High
The BBB disclaimer works against the site.
The site includes a note saying the BBB "only accredits the business management of a school, not the quality of the curriculum." This sentence plants a seed of doubt. If you list the BBB logo, let it stand alone. Do not explain what it doesn't cover.
Medium
Financial assistance is available but buried.
"We can help you pay for this" removes the #1 reason people don't enroll. It belongs on line one of the enrollment section — not three scrolls down the page.
The Transformation Gap

Every strong vocational school selling copy answers one question: What does my life look like six months from now if I do this?

The ATS site never answers it. It lists what programs teach — not what graduates earn, who hires them, or how fast they get placed. That gap is where prospective students drop off.

Revenue Audit · Score: 22/100

Where Money Is Being Left Behind

ATS has a natural three-level upsell ladder in its crane programs. It is not being used. And there is no pricing anywhere — which means every visitor has to ask before they can decide.

Critical
No pricing on the homepage or program pages
Visitors cannot self-qualify. The cost of a vocational program is the #1 question before enrollment. When pricing is hidden, serious buyers assume it's expensive and move on. Financial assistance is available — but without a price anchor, it has nothing to offset. Display starting price or a price range with financing options.
Critical
The Level 1→2→3 crane certification ladder is a natural revenue multiplier — it's invisible
ATS offers three crane certification levels. A student who starts at Level 1 (hydraulic cranes) has a reason to return for Level 2 (lattice boom) and Level 3 (tower cranes). This is a built-in upsell path that increases lifetime value per student. The site lists these as separate programs rather than a career progression. Reframe it as "The ATS Crane Career Path" and watch repeat enrollment rise.
High
The Free Starter Kit is a strong lead magnet — buried among 5 other CTAs
A free resource that helps someone decide to enroll is one of the highest-ROI tools in education marketing. When it competes with Photo Gallery and Contact Us at equal weight, its value is diluted. Make it the single above-fold CTA. Build an email list. Follow up. Every email address is a future enrollment conversation.
High
Employment outcome data ($26/hr wages, 50+ yr track record) is not near any CTA
Buyer psychology: people make purchase decisions when the future benefit outweighs the present cost. The wages graduates earn and the employers who hire them should appear immediately before every enrollment CTA. Right now, that proof is at the bottom in small testimonial text.

30-Day Roadmap

Fix the Foundation First

These are the changes that stop the bleeding. None require a full site rebuild. All can be done in the existing CMS with a copywriter and a front-end developer working together for two to three weeks.

Week 1 — Copy & Messaging
Write the new hero headline and subheadline. Focus on outcome: career, wages, timeline. Test two versions if possible.
Select the 3 best testimonials with specific outcomes. Wages, job titles, time to placement. These become rotating proof near every CTA.
Rewrite all "Click here" links. Every link should describe the destination. One afternoon of edits, real SEO and conversion lift.
Remove the BBB disclaimer text. Keep the logo. Delete the sentence that explains what the BBB does not cover.
Week 2 — Navigation & CTAs
Collapse navigation to 6 items. Programs · Apply · Financing · Reviews · About · Contact. All other pages become sub-pages.
Replace 6 homepage CTAs with 1 primary + 1 secondary. "Get Your Free Career Starter Kit" (primary) and "Apply Now" (text link below).
Move "Hiring Instructors" off the homepage. Create a dedicated Careers page. Link it from About Us, not from the student hero.
Week 3–4 — Trust & Mobile
Add a trust bar directly below the hero CTA. NCCCO logo · "50+ Years" · "Veteran Friendly" · "Financing Available." One line, no paragraph.
Add starting prices to program pages. Even a range ("starting from $X — financing available") removes hidden-price anxiety.
Fix the worst mobile breakpoints. At minimum: CTA buttons full-width on mobile, font sizes ≥16px throughout, no horizontal scroll.
Set up basic email capture on the Starter Kit download. First name + email only. Every subscriber is a future enrollment conversation.

90-Day Growth Roadmap

Build the Engine

Once the foundation is solid, these moves build a system that brings in students and keeps them moving through the program ladder — without more ad spend.

Revenue Expansion
Launch "The ATS Crane Career Path" page. One page showing the Level 1→2→3 progression with wages at each stage. This is the upsell funnel that already exists in the catalog — it just needs a page.
Add a program comparison table. Heavy Equipment vs. Crane vs. CDL — duration, cost, average starting wage, certification earned. Help visitors self-select the right program.
Build an employer partners section. "We place graduates with 500+ employers nationwide." Name the top 5–10 companies. Employment certainty is a buying decision, not a bonus.
Add a cohort calendar with open seats. "Next class starts August 12. 4 seats remaining." Urgency that is honest — because it is real.
Lead Generation & SEO
Write 4 targeted blog posts. "How much does a crane operator make in Wisconsin?" · "NCCCO certification: what it is and why employers want it" · "Heavy equipment vs. CDL: which is right for you?" · "How to pay for trade school." Each one answers a search question and leads to a program page.
Add FAQ schema to the site. Structured data lets Google show your answers in search results. AI tools also pull from this. Costs one afternoon to implement.
Set up a 5-email welcome sequence for Starter Kit subscribers. Email 1: Kit delivery. Email 2: Graduate story + wages. Email 3: Financing options. Email 4: FAQ answers. Email 5: Enrollment invitation. This is an automated enrollment conversation.
Claim and optimize the Google Business Profile. Local search for "heavy equipment training near Sun Prairie WI" should surface ATS in map results. Free. Takes one hour.

The long game: Every graduate who gets placed is a referral waiting to happen. Build a simple referral program. "Send us a student, get a $500 voucher toward your next certification." Your graduates become your best recruiters.
